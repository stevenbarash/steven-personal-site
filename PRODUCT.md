# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The site serves hiring managers, technical leaders, potential collaborators, and professional peers who are evaluating Steven Barash's professional background, technical work, creative practice, and personality. Visitors should be able to understand his range quickly, inspect relevant projects or experience, and find a direct path to connect.

## Product Purpose

This is a distinctive personal portfolio that brings together solutions engineering and identity expertise, independent software projects, professional experience, and photography. It should make Steven memorable, demonstrate credible technical depth, and help visitors continue into the part of his work that matters to them: projects, resume, photography, or contact links.

Success means the Windows 95 concept feels functional rather than decorative, the visitor can locate relevant material without decoding the interface, and the experience creates a strong reason to remember or contact Steven.

## Brand Personality

Nostalgic, technically credible, and playful. The Windows 95 desktop is the permanent core identity: recognizable and characterful, but still purposeful enough to carry serious professional content.

## Anti-references

- Generic personal-site templates that could belong to anyone.
- Sterile SaaS dashboards or polished corporate marketing pages that erase Steven's personality.
- Contemporary glass, gradient, oversized-radius, or card-grid styling that breaks the Windows 95 visual language.
- Decorative nostalgia whose controls, labels, and interaction promises do not actually work.
- Forced retro behavior that makes the mobile experience frustrating.

## Design Principles

1. Make the metaphor functional. Desktop, window, menu, icon, and taskbar behaviors should help visitors navigate instead of acting as decoration.
2. Make technical credibility easy to verify. Projects and experience should show substance, outcomes, and working links without losing the playful presentation.
3. Preserve personality without obscuring purpose. The Windows 95 identity should make the site memorable while keeping projects, resume, photography, and contact paths obvious.
4. Respect each device. Desktop may use authentic spatial and multi-step interactions; mobile should use direct, one-tap controls and layouts sized for touch.
5. Prefer specific details over portfolio filler. Every label, project description, and interaction should say something concrete about Steven or his work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG conformance target is required. Mobile interactions must be touch friendly: do not require double-clicking, use practical tap targets, prevent clipped or unreadably dense content, and preserve clear state changes. Retain sensible keyboard behavior where it already exists or can be supported without undermining the experience.
